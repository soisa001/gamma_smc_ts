# Demographic history, introgression, and selection in the All of Us long-read panel

Working Methods and Results draft, 28 September 2026. Selection simulations are excluded.

**Editorial status.** The Methods below describe the empirical analysis implemented in the local repositories, with the requested selection statistic explicitly specified as **P(TMRCA < 50,000 years)**. They are a manuscript draft, not an assertion that every implemented analysis has completed. Numerical Results are restricted to the supplied local summary tables and validated aggregate PHLASH artifacts. Bracketed fields require the final production summaries; they are not zero or negative findings. The source audit in `evidence_and_open_items.md` identifies older settings, unavailable outputs, and the evidence supporting each result. In particular, the available Gamma-SMC locus table uses a different, 4,500-year hard-call statistic and cannot supply results for the requested analysis. The demographic addition distinguishes implemented workflow settings from the parameters recoverable for the saved fits.

## Methods

### Cohort, genomic inputs, and analysis denominators

We analyzed phased autosomal variation from the All of Us (AoU) phase-2 long-read whole-genome sequencing panel on GRCh38. Population strata were assigned using the v9 ancestry table and comprised AFR, AMR, EAS, EUR, MID, and SAS, with OTH retained as a separate descriptive stratum in the introgression analyses. These labels denote analysis groups derived from genetic ancestry predictions. The final analysis included [N_INTROGRESSION] individuals with successful canonical introgression decodes and v9 membership, including [N_AFR], [N_AMR], [N_EAS], [N_EUR], [N_MID], [N_SAS], and [N_OTH] individuals in the respective strata. The precise QC and relatedness exclusions were taken from the recorded run configuration rather than inferred from the presence of exclusion files.

The introgression analysis denominator was defined from successful decoder records, intersected with the v9 inclusion list and the run's recorded exclusions. Individuals with successful decoding but no retained archaic tract remained in the denominator. Failed or missing decodes and separately published posterior-fallback calls were not treated as successful zero-tract observations. The trained, AFR donor-model, and OTH callsets were reconciled to a single nonduplicated cohort. OTH contributed to pooled summaries and its own population summaries, but did not establish or veto specificity to one of the six canonical populations.

For Gamma-SMC, the empirical runner separately intersected ancestry assignments with the sample order in each chromosome BCF, retained the six canonical populations, and removed samples listed in the QC or relatedness exclusion tables. OTH was not included in this scan. Consequently, the introgression and selection cohorts were not assumed to be identical; sample counts and the shared cohort for integrated analyses must be reported from their respective manifests. The final selection scan comprised [N_SELECTION_BY_POPULATION] individuals.

### Demographic inference with PHLASH

Population-specific histories of diploid effective population size, N_e(t), were estimated using PHLASH (population history learning by averaging sampled histories). PHLASH infers a distribution over piecewise-constant coalescent-rate histories using a PSMC-like hidden Markov model and frequency-spectrum information; its particle-based Stein variational gradient descent procedure approximates Bayesian inference. For a coalescence rate c(t) expressed per generation, the local implementation reports N_e(t) = 1/[2c(t)]. We used the locally modified PHLASH 1.0.6 workflow, which additionally supports a two-locus linkage-disequilibrium (LD) component. This extension and its settings are study-specific, rather than features established by citing the original method alone. [Terhorst, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02323-x).

The AoU demographic workflow constructs separate AFR, AMR, EAS, EUR, MID, and SAS panels by joining long-read BCF sample identifiers to the v9 `ancestry_pred_other` assignments and excluding every identifier in the configured v9 flagged-sample table. OTH is omitted. Eligible samples must occur in every requested autosomal genotype store. Input conversion retains biallelic SNVs and genotype calls, removes indels, multiallelic and structural-variant records, and stores the data in indexed VCZ/Zarr format. The demographic analysis uses genotype heterozygosity and a folded allele-frequency spectrum; unlike HMMIX's derived-allele observations, this workflow does not require ancestral-state polarization.

The reviewed workflow restricts analysis to intergenic sequence by trimming 1 Mb from both chromosome ends and excluding coding-gene loci plus 100-kb flanks, using the bundled GRCh38 RefSeq annotation. Each excluded locus spans the outer boundaries of all its transcripts. Remaining spans of at least 10 kb form separate contigs. The LD bootstrap launcher explicitly enables the configured `hardmask.hg38.v4.over99.bed` exclusion mask. Under the reviewed 20% maximum masked-fraction setting, retained contigs can contain internal masked gaps; those positions are omitted from variant input and modeled as missing. The launcher also specifies `max_missing_sites=0`, so any base covered by the supplied interval mask makes its 100-bp HMM window missing. Missing genotype calls are excluded from heterozygote detection but do not, by themselves, mark the whole window missing in the reviewed VCZ loader. The longest retained contig is held out for predictive-density monitoring. Exact retained sequence length and the historical mask choice remain [PHLASH_RETAINED_BASES_AND_MASK_FROM_RUN].

PHLASH observation windows are fixed physical intervals anchored at the start of each retained intergenic contig. For a zero-based contig [a, b), window j is [a + 100j, min(a + 100(j + 1), b)); a variant at zero-based position p is assigned to floor((p - a)/100). Thus, the final window can be shorter than 100 bp, and window boundaries restart for each contig. Each individual/window is encoded as 1 if at least one unmasked, fully called heterozygous SNV is observed, 0 otherwise, or -1 if the interval-mask rule excludes the window. Multiple heterozygous SNVs within one window still produce a single 1. A zero records absence of an observed heterozygote, not proof that every base was confidently homozygous. HMM chunks span 1,100 observation windows and advance by 1,000 windows; the first 100 windows initialize the hidden-state distribution and the remaining 1,000 supply the scored observations. The first 10 kb of a contig therefore acts as warm-up under this launcher setting, and chunk tails beyond the contig are padded with missing observations.

For uncertainty assessment, the resampling workflow selects 100 distinct diploid individuals without replacement within each population and repeats this selection 100 times, allowing individuals to recur across fits. This procedure is repeated sample subsampling, called "bootstrap" in the software, rather than a classical with-replacement individual bootstrap or a genomic block bootstrap. Sample manifests preserve each fit's IDs and seed. The reviewed LD launcher uses base seed 20260724; population and replicate seeds are derived deterministically, and persisted sample manifests take precedence on restart. The saved aggregate artifacts retain 100 fit summaries per population, but do not themselves prove the historical sample membership or sample count of each fit.

The reviewed optimized launcher specifies up to 2,000 SVGD iterations, 200 particles, learning rate 0.01, and 100-bp HMM windows organized into 1,000-window chunks with 100 warm-up windows. The HMM minibatch size is five individual-by-contig chunks sampled per optimization step, with replacement. Each row contains nominally 100 kb of modeled sequence plus 10 kb of preceding warm-up sequence; terminal padding and masked windows contribute missing observations. These five chunk rows are shared across the 200 particle updates, and are distinct from the 100-individual fit size. The total-row/minibatch correction is applied to the HMM term. Both training and held-out HMM sample caps are set to the requested 100 diploids. The folded AFS and HMM components are supplemented by normalized two-locus D-squared/pi-squared summaries from contigs at least 1 Mb long, evaluated in 320-kb blocks. The five LD bins have recombination-distance edges 0.00005, 0.0001, 0.0002, 0.0004, 0.0008, and 0.0016 Morgans. These correspond to 5--160 kb only under a uniform 1e-8 Morgans/bp rate; the configured analysis instead uses the sex-averaged deCODE GRCh38 map of [Halldorsson et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau1043), distributed by UCSC as `hg38/recombRate/recombAvg.bw`. The launcher requests 10-kb mean-rate bins, converts cM/Mb to Morgans/bp by multiplying by 1e-8, and uses a 1e-8 Morgans/bp fallback for absent chromosomes or missing bigWig-bin values. [UCSC track documentation](https://www.genome.ucsc.edu/cgi-bin/hgTrackUi?db=hg38&g=recombRate2). The LD minibatch size is also five: all five distance bins are evaluated at every optimization step, up to 100 diploids, and a cap of 1,000,000 variant pairs per block/bin. The prior, HMM, AFS, and LD objective multipliers are 1, 1, 1, and 20, respectively. These weighted, dependent data summaries form a composite objective, so the LD term should not be described as an independent replication of the HMM evidence.

**Run-provenance note:** These are the settings of the reviewed launcher, not a recovered invocation for all 600 archived fits. The aggregate sidecars identify source fits under `phlash_bootstrap_trial_1mb_2000i_200p`, but do not include the fit metadata, sample manifests, stopping diagnostics, or full invocation. The launcher supplies a mutation rate of 1.29e-8 per base per generation, whereas the generic Python caller defaults to 1.25e-8. The historical value must therefore be confirmed from [PHLASH_FIT_METADATA_AND_INVOCATION], together with masking, LD settings, actual iterations, sample counts, and retained sequence. The Results below preserve the archived generation and N_e scales without rescaling them to an assumed mutation rate. A calendar-time axis requires an explicitly stated generation interval; at the Gamma-SMC convention of 25 years/generation, 2,000 generations corresponds to 50,000 years.

### Demographic summaries and between-fit uncertainty

For each resampling fit, the postprocessing code evaluates every fitted posterior history on a common 10,000-point geometric grid spanning 100--40,000 generations and takes the pointwise posterior median. The published artifact contains the resulting 100 by 10,000 matrix for each population. We summarized this matrix using the median and 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles across the 100 fit-level curves, using linear quantile interpolation. Values reported at specified times use the nearest stored grid point. These intervals quantify between-fit variation in posterior medians; they neither pool the within-fit posterior particles nor constitute simultaneous 95% confidence bands. The dense evaluation grid does not imply 10,000 independently resolved demographic epochs.

The accompanying compact multivariate-normal (MVN) representation is fitted to log N_e. With L_rj = log N_e,r(t_j), the saved mean is m_j = mean_r L_rj and covariance factor is F_rj = (L_rj - m_j)/sqrt(99), giving covariance F-transpose F of rank at most 99. All six artifacts specify zero added log-space jitter. This representation summarizes covariance across fit-level histories; its lognormal marginal medians and intervals need not equal empirical quantiles of the 100 archived curves. We report the empirical fit-level summaries here. Artifact integrity was checked using ZIP CRCs, SHA-256 identities, schema and shape checks, finite positive curves, a common time grid, complete `fit000`--`fit099` source listings, and reconstruction of the log-space mean and covariance factor. Original fits were not rerun. No inference about the present or times outside the archived grid is obtained by extending its endpoints.

### HMMIX observation construction and callability

Candidate archaic tracts were inferred with hmmix 0.9.1, using a two-state hidden Markov model to distinguish ordinary modern-human sequence from regions enriched for variants absent from an unadmixed outgroup. This discovery approach does not require an archaic reference genome; archaic references were used subsequently to characterize tract affinity. [Skov et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1007641).

Chromosome-specific panel BCFs were converted to per-individual, phase-resolved observations using the matched GRCh38 ancestral sequence and the precomputed 1000 Genomes/HGDP outgroup resource. Positions represented in the outgroup were excluded. Split multiallelic representations were rejoined before genotype-to-base conversion to preserve the two haplotypes correctly. Observation files were checked for valid format, genomic order, phasing, chromosome coverage, and representation of both haplotypes before training and decoding.

The HMM used 1-kb windows, with callable-sequence weights and a local mutation-rate track supplied by the matched HMMIX resource bundle. The primary introgression analyses used the positive GRCh38 strict-callability mask. An independently supplied exclusion mask, `hardmask.hg38.v4.over99.bed`, was evaluated through its autosomal complement as a callability sensitivity analysis. These masks have different meanings and were not interchanged. Genomic interval calculations and BED outputs used zero-based, half-open coordinates; VCF positions and literature coordinates were converted explicitly when joined to these intervals.

### Model fitting, Artemis optimization, and haplotype decoding

For normally trained non-AFR individuals, a separate haploid-mode model was fitted using the two phased haplotypes jointly. The production launcher allowed up to 2,000 training iterations. Each eligible individual then underwent Artemis optimization on a 101-point alpha grid, followed by hybrid decoding with its own selected alpha. Hybrid decoding combines local posterior support with a globally coherent state path. [Coll Macià et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msag134).

The implementation reconstructed alpha from the emitted Artemis scores: average pointwise posterior probability and path log likelihood were each rescaled to the unit interval, and the grid point with the smallest distance to the diagonal was selected, with ties resolved by the first grid row. The implementation specifically handled nonfinite endpoint likelihoods arising from numerical underflow; invalid interior scores remained failures. The selected alpha, fitted parameters, successful decoder, and any fallback status were retained for each individual. Population-level alpha distributions were used for diagnostics. The older design based on 100 calibrators per population and population-median alpha was a separate supported configuration, and is not assumed here to describe the final all-sample production run.

AFR individuals were decoded without fitting an independent archaic-detection model to each target. Instead, the workflow constructed an effective model from the component-wise median of eligible EUR fitted models and optimized Artemis alpha on each target's observations. For OTH, the current mixed strategy fitted individuals whose canonical ancestry prediction was non-AFR on their own data and used the EUR median for AFR-predicted individuals. OTH individuals predicted EUR did not contribute to the EUR donor-model median. The repaired snapshot also retained a documented single-sample AMR donor-model override while preserving that sample's OTH label. Effective model checksums and override records, rather than current defaults, identify the model used for a historical decode. These donor-model and self-trained outputs were retained as distinguishable analysis strata.

### Posterior filtering and Neanderthal/Denisovan affinity

Decoded intervals were retained as supported introgression when the HMM state was archaic, the stored segment-mean posterior probability was at least 0.90, and the tract had sufficient affinity to a sequenced archaic lineage. The posterior filter was inclusive and applied to the segment posterior rounded to five decimal places in the normalized output. It was not a requirement that every 1-kb HMM window individually exceed 0.90.

For each haplotype tract, we compared its derived observations with Altai, Vindija, and Chagyrskaya Neanderthals and with Denisova. Neanderthal affinity was the number of derived observations matching at least one of the three Neanderthal genomes divided by all derived observations on that haplotype within the tract. Denisovan affinity used the corresponding Denisova match count and the same denominator. A site matching multiple Neanderthal genomes counted once in the Neanderthal numerator. The denominator was not restricted to archaic-matching or jointly callable archaic sites.

Let A_N and A_D denote Neanderthal and Denisovan affinity. A tract was classified as NEA when A_N >= 0.30 and A_N > 1.5 A_D, and as DEN when A_D >= 0.30 and A_D > 1.5 A_N. Tracts with both affinities >= 0.30 but no strict 1.5-fold winner were classified as SHARED, termed Shared High Affinity. Remaining tracts were LOW_EVIDENCE. Total supported introgression included NEA, DEN, and SHARED; lineage-specific analyses used NEA and DEN separately. LOW_EVIDENCE tracts were retained for diagnostics but excluded from supported burden and primary overlap analyses. No minimum count of 30 informative sites was imposed by this classification rule. Affinity classes describe similarity conditional on a detected tract and do not identify an admixture pulse or prove that SHARED sequence derives from an unknown archaic population.

### Introgression burden, genomic coverage, and regional frequency

For each haplotype, introgression burden was the number of supported tract base pairs within the callable genome divided by callable base pairs. Population and pooled burden used the corresponding haplotype-base-pair denominators, including successfully decoded haplotypes with zero supported sequence. Cohort-wide genomic coverage was calculated separately as the union of callable supported intervals across all included haplotypes divided by the callable genome. Thus, burden measured sequence carried per haplotype, whereas union coverage measured the genomic breadth represented anywhere in the cohort. Saturation curves accumulated the supported interval union in the workflow's deterministic haplotype order and checked the final union against the independently calculated coverage.

Regional maps used nonoverlapping 50-kb bins. For each population and source class, the workflow counted distinct carrier haplotypes and distinct carrier individuals; an individual carrying a tract on either or both haplotypes contributed once to the individual-carrier count. Multiple tracts on one haplotype did not increase its bin count beyond one. Frequencies used the successful decoded cohort, rather than only individuals with at least one archaic tract. Bins with less than 50% callable sequence were excluded from high-frequency regional calls.

The standard regional analysis merged adjacent eligible bins with haplotype-carrier frequency greater than 25%. The separate functional screen selected positive-frequency bins at or above the population- and source-specific 95th percentile of individual-carrier frequency, retaining ties; this need not select exactly 5% of bins. Standard cohort-defined deserts had no supported tract, spanned at least 1 Mb after trimming noncallable edges, and contained no more than 10% internal noncallable sequence. The optional landscape analysis used its own 500-kb desert and 20% peak defaults and was not pooled with the standard definitions. Published deserts and curated loci were compared in the common GRCh38 coordinate system. Actual threshold overrides remain to be verified from the final run.

### Genic and functional analyses

Genic, exonic, and intronic tract overlap was evaluated using the staged gene annotations. Segment-based enrichment compared the observed feature coverage with random intervals matched for tract length and masked fraction; the implemented default was 1,000 permutations with a masked-fraction tolerance of 0.05. Tracts for which a matched placement could not be obtained were removed from both observed and null calculations. Repeated copies of a tract were distinguished from unique intervals: the optional coordinate-collapse setting changes the unit of analysis and must be specified with any inferential result. Regional frequency analyses also implemented chromosome-wise circular shifts to evaluate alignment with genic annotations while retaining the spatial structure of the frequency track.

For the high-introgression-bin screen, two-by-two tables compared any overlap with each supplied functional track in selected versus remaining eligible bins. The workflow reported uncorrected and Haldane-Anscombe odds ratios, two-sided Fisher exact P values, and Benjamini-Hochberg q values across the tested population/source/track combinations. These bin-based tests were descriptive because nearby bins are correlated. RefSeq, GENCODE, and ENCODE annotations retained their separate versions and roles; an exon overlap from one annotation was not automatically promoted to a coding consequence in another.

### Structural variants associated with introgressed haplotypes

Structural variants were evaluated as exact alternate alleles, preserving distinct alleles at a shared VCF record or coordinate. For sequence-resolved alleles, common REF/ALT prefixes and suffixes were removed to define the changed-reference span. A pure insertion was represented by its interbase breakpoint. A carrier allele was associated with introgression only when its complete changed-reference span, or its insertion point, lay within one supported tract on the same phased haplotype. Adjacent tracts were not stitched to satisfy this criterion. Net insertion or deletion length was the absolute REF/ALT length difference; for a complex replacement, this was not the width of the reference interval affected.

Two complementary analyses were implemented. The segment-based analysis compared SV presence within introgressed tracts with the same genomic intervals on matched haplotypes from a different individual in the same population, excluding controls with overlapping supported introgression. Its default used 1,000 matched-control draws and within-pair case/control swaps. The reciprocal event-based analysis compared introgression at an exact SV allele among its phase-resolved carriers and noncarriers using a one-sided Fisher enrichment test. An exclusive-carrier event had no observed phase-resolved ALT carrier outside a containing supported tract. This was a within-cohort association definition, not proof of archaic mutational origin or adaptive introgression.

Primary callability, tandem-repeat/homopolymer, segmental-duplication, and custom-hardmask flags were retained. In the reviewed exclusive-carrier ranking these flags evaluated the original variant anchor; passing them did not establish that an entire complex breakpoint span was callable. Historical sparse carrier caches did not reliably distinguish missing genotypes from reference noncarriers. The newer complete-genotype sidecars separately count the exact alternate allele and all called alleles, allowing AF = AC/AN without replacing missing alleles by reference calls. Phase-unresolved alternate copies may contribute to AC and AN but cannot be assigned to an introgressed haplotype without phase. These updated denominators do not retroactively validate older sparse-cache estimates.

For the selected exclusive-SV review, allele-trimmed coordinates were annotated against GENCODE v47 on GRCh38, retaining transcript, exon, CDS, UTR, and MANE Select information. Point insertions used the one-base annotation query [p,p+1). Selected coding/exonic candidates were cross-checked against local RefSeq annotations. Gene or exon overlap was treated as a functional hypothesis; full allele sequence and transcript-specific consequences are required to establish a protein-altering effect.

### Gamma-SMC selection scan using P(TMRCA < 50,000 years)

We specified a population-stratified screen for excess recent pairwise coalescence using Gamma-SMC. Gamma-SMC approximates the posterior distribution of the time to the most recent common ancestor (TMRCA) for each haplotype pair at each genomic position with a continuous gamma distribution. [Schweiger and Durbin, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.277665.123).

The empirical workflow sampled 100,000 distinct unordered haplotype pairs uniformly within each population using a fixed seed of 1729. Identical-haplotype self-pairs and duplicate unordered pairs were rejected; pairs comprising the two homologues of one individual remained eligible under the default configuration. This design differs from decoding only one within-individual pair per person. Pair identities and the BCF-ordered sample manifest were saved for reproducibility. Shared haplotypes across pairs mean that these 100,000 observations are not statistically independent.

Inference used phased biallelic segregating SNPs from each single-chromosome BCF. Indels and structural variants were excluded from the Gamma-SMC emissions and were overlaid afterward for candidate interpretation. Unphased heterozygous SNPs were rejected, while missing alleles were handled as missing emissions. Posterior summaries were evaluated at a fixed 10-kb grid, with a 1-kb transition cache. The empirical configuration used scaled mutation rate theta = 0.00075, mutation rate mu = 1.29 x 10^-8 per base per generation, rho/theta = 0.8, and a 25-year generation interval. These are verified code defaults, pending confirmation against the final empirical run record. The main runner used the complement of the supplied exclusion mask; its strict-callability mode used the positive HMMIX mask as a separate analysis. The final manuscript must identify which mask supplied the primary selection scan.

The requested primary statistic is the mean posterior probability of coalescence within 50,000 years:

\[
S_p(x)=\frac{1}{M_p(x)}\sum_{j=1}^{M_p(x)}
P\!\left(T_{p,j}(x)<50{,}000\ {\rm years}\mid D_{p,j}\right),
\]

where D denotes the sequence observations and M_p(x) is the number of valid pairwise posterior estimates in population p at position x. At 25 years per generation, the threshold is 2,000 generations. With Gamma-SMC's coalescent time scale, c = theta/(2 mu) = 29,069.7674 generations, so the dimensionless threshold is u = 2,000/c = 0.0688. For posterior shape alpha and **rate** beta, each probability is

\[
P(T<50{,}000\mid D)=F_{\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)}(0.0688)
=\frac{\gamma(\alpha,\beta\,0.0688)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.
\]

Here gamma denotes the lower incomplete gamma function. The software's `mean_p_tmrca_lt_threshold` column is the corresponding average when the requested threshold is configured as 50,000 years; an explicitly threshold-named probability column can be used when multiple thresholds are emitted. The optimized decoder evaluates these probabilities through lookup-table interpolation, with an exact gamma-CDF mode available for numerical validation. Time scaling follows the [Gamma-SMC documentation](https://github.com/regevs/gamma_smc).

This statistic is distinct from the fraction of pairs whose posterior mean TMRCA is below 50,000 years, and from the fraction whose posterior probability exceeds an additional cutoff. Neither hard-call quantity is substituted for S_p(x). Elevated S_p(x) indicates more posterior mass on recent shared ancestry and is the direction of the intended sweep screen. It is not a frequentist selection P value, does not date the onset of selection or introgression, and may be affected by demography, admixture, phasing, and local data quality. In particular, pairwise coalescence between modern carriers can occur long after the divergence of archaic and modern lineages.

The final 50,000-year candidate threshold and selection rule are [CANDIDATE_RULE_50K], to be specified from the completed empirical scan. The older default of a >2% hard-call fraction at 4,500 years is not transferable to this posterior-probability statistic. If the existing interval-merging convention is retained, selected 10-kb windows are combined when the intervening gap is at most 20 kb. The optional 1-Mb joining used for figure labels is only a display operation and does not define an independent biological locus. Gene annotations provide positional context, not causal assignments. No simulation-derived significance threshold, false-positive rate, or power estimate is included here.

### Integration of selection and introgression

Population-specific Gamma-SMC candidate intervals were designed to be overlaid with the corresponding population's supported-introgression landscape. In the implemented enrichment module, eligible 50-kb bins are classified by overlap with a same-population candidate interval and by whether at least one individual carries supported introgression. A one-sided Fisher exact test compares introgression presence inside and outside candidate intervals; analogous tests compare functional-track overlap. Two-sided P values and Haldane-Anscombe odds ratios are retained, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied separately to the population-level introgression family and the population-by-track functional family.

This integration tests spatial association, with no permutation calibration in that module. Adjacent bins are correlated, and the selection and introgression analyses may differ in cohort and callable masks. Candidate selection, cohort compatibility, and mask overlap therefore require explicit verification before interpretation. Co-localization of a selection candidate and an archaic tract is insufficient to establish that selection favored the archaic haplotype or a particular SV allele. The existing placeholder selection-region file is not an input for final 50,000-year claims.

### Reproducibility

Analyses use locked uv environments and phased execution with manifests, effective parameter files, command logs, software revisions, and source/output checksums. Successful completion and cache compatibility are checked against the cohort, inputs, relevant settings, and required outputs. Compressed tables are checked for stream integrity and valid records. Existing deterministic seeds are retained; this manuscript review introduced no stochastic calculations. Plots are generated headlessly with the Agg backend and saved to PNG and vector PDF with editable text, without notebook or inline display. The present review used only local files and public method references and did not retrieve controlled cloud data.

### Optional LCT linkage analysis

For the supporting LCT analysis, ancestry-stratified two-locus genotype counts were used to estimate the four insertion/SNP haplotype frequencies by expectation-maximization under random haplotype pairing within each group. The implementation evaluated nine deterministic starting coupling fractions (0, 10^-6, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 1-10^-6, and 1), used tolerance 10^-12 and a maximum of 10,000 iterations per start, and compared converged solutions by the full unphased genotype likelihood. Distinct equal-likelihood solutions were flagged. D, signed and absolute D-prime, and haplotypic r-squared were derived from the estimated frequencies. The SNP allele was explicitly rs4988235 forward-strand A. These estimates were distinguished from observed phased LD and from squared genotype-dosage correlation; uncertainty intervals and genotype re-extraction were not available in the supplied summary.

## Results

### PHLASH histories show population differences in inferred effective size

The archived demographic output comprised **600 fit-level posterior-median histories**, with 100 for each of the six population strata, evaluated at 10,000 shared time points from 100 to 40,000 generations. All six NPZ files matched their published checksums, had finite positive histories, and reproduced their saved log-space means and covariance factors within floating-point tolerance. These checks establish integrity of the available summaries; convergence and fit to the original genotype summaries require the original diagnostics.

At the stored grid point closest to 2,000 generations (1,999.401 generations), the median inferred N_e was **20,173 in AFR**, compared with **3,632--4,590** across AMR, EAS, EUR, MID, and SAS (Table 1). Under a 25-year generation interval this grid point lies at approximately 49,985 years, near the time horizon used for the Gamma-SMC recent-coalescence statistic.

The AMR, EAS, EUR, MID, and SAS median histories each reached their grid-wide minimum between approximately **1,277 and 2,122 generations**, with minimum N_e values of **3,139--4,425**. At 100 generations their median estimates were higher, ranging from **34,731 to 42,148**; the corresponding AFR estimate was **55,964**. These curves describe an inferred reduction in effective size followed by expansion toward the recent end of the archived interval. They do not establish the timing of a discrete demographic event: the quoted minima are extrema of pointwise median curves, and uncertainty in their timing has not been estimated. In particular, the 100-generation values are endpoint estimates rather than contemporary population sizes.

The population differences provide demographic context for comparing recent-coalescence signals across ancestry strata. N_e(t) is an effective size under the fitted coalescent model, not a census count; population structure and admixture can also affect its trajectory. This qualification is especially relevant to the AMR stratum and precludes reading its single-population curve as a complete admixture history. PHLASH summaries alone do not calibrate a locus-specific selection significance threshold, and the temporal overlap with the 50,000-year Gamma-SMC horizon does not establish selection. Selection simulations are excluded from this draft.

The supplied compact SV table contained **38,219 distinct allele-specific events**, comprising **25,150 insertions and 13,069 deletions** by net allele-length change. For every row, the reported allele count equaled the reported number of containing introgressed segments. This agreement describes the export's recorded phase-resolved support; it does not establish complete genotype callability or a population allele frequency.

The supplied annotations identified gene overlap for **14,116 events**, exon overlap for **1,213**, intron overlap for **12,282**, and at least one candidate cis-regulatory element for **26,444**. The table lacks called-allele denominators and the full run-level inclusion history, so these counts describe the supplied candidate export rather than an estimate of all introgressed SVs in the population. Exon and regulatory-element annotations prioritize follow-up but do not demonstrate functional effects.
