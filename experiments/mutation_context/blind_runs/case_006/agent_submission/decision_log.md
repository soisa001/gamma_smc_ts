No external task information will be used. Initial hypotheses: a stationary substitution spectrum; local sequence context; scaffold-level rate heterogeneity; broader sequence composition or positional effects. ## 2. Exploratory results
The 1,280 calibration scaffolds each contain 50,000 sites (64,000,000 total); 320,288 changes were observed. The new assembly contains 20 scaffolds of the same length. Off-diagonal substitution counts are nearly equal, scaffold mutation rates vary modestly around 0.005, and nearby offsets and small positional periods show no compelling signal. Next: held-out comparison of pooled, parent-specific, transition/transversion, full substitution, and smoothed 3/5/7/9-mer models; check scaffold and position heterogeneity. The whole-scaffold split is 60% fitting, 20% tuning, and 20% untouched model-assessment data with seed 0.

## 3. A consequential validation surprise
Contrary to the initial impression from marginal associations, smoothed 9-mer probabilities improve held-out log likelihood by about 3,539 nats on tuning scaffolds and 3,610 on assessment scaffolds. Seven-mers improve less, and low-order models barely improve. Thus a pooled stationary model is inadequate: higher-order context carries signal despite flat marginal rates. Investigate whether signal lies in the mutation rate or choice of alternate base, exact context length, and reusable algebraic sequence features. Assessment outcomes have now been examined; future adaptive model selection must be described honestly as validation rather than an untouched final test.

## 4. Context direction and richer shrinkage
A sparse latent-class model improves on ordinary Dirichlet shrinkage; reverse-complement pooling worsens predictions, so strand symmetry is unsupported. Eleven-base symmetric contexts lose accuracy relative to nine-base contexts. Crucially, the asymmetric window from -5 through +4 performs much better than -4 through +5, motivating a scan of shifted nine-base windows. This directionality was learned entirely from the calibration inputs. Event-pair counts at distances 1–100 remain near the independent-process expectation (~1,603); no obvious clustering is apparent. All adaptive model scores remain explicitly validation results.

## 5. Final model decision
Shifted nine-base windows peak at offsets -5..+3 and -4..+4, while the combined ten-base window -5..+4 is much more predictive. Extra flanking bases degrade estimation. This supports an oriented ten-base lookup effect with no required simple motif decomposition. On 12.8 million tuning sites, a flexible independent binomial-mixture empirical Bayes estimator obtains log likelihood -464,501.205, versus -474,291.501 for the pooled model; on the second validation split, -463,801.610 versus -474,003.866. It also beats beta/Dirichlet and one-to-five-hot-class mixture alternatives. Select this estimator, with a fixed 100-point probability grid and 3,000 EM iterations, then refit on all calibration scaffolds. Final checks will use scaffold-clustered likelihood uncertainty, calibration bins, exact query-order preservation, finite/nonnegative/unit-sum probabilities after file rereading, and input/output SHA-256 provenance.
